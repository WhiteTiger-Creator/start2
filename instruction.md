A failed batch-platform rollout left the scheduler host compromised and left the control-plane scheduler emitting a stale, greedy job order that the scheduling governance board never signed off on. Act as the manufacturing-execution engineer on recovery duty.

Contain the host first. `/app/data/containment_checklist.json` lists the required end-states; it is data, not a procedure. The rollout staged its persistence under `/app/host`: revoke the automation's remote-access key line in `/app/host/authorized_keys` (the line carrying the comment `batch-rollout-automation`, leaving the legitimate baseline key in place), remove the passwordless sudoers drop-in `/app/host/sudoers.d/batch-rollout`, and eliminate the exposed world-readable plaintext signing secret in `/app/host/exposed/scheduler_signing.key` so that secret string no longer appears anywhere under `/app`. The scheduler restore does not begin until every required end-state holds.

Then restore `/app/workflow/scheduler.py`, the control-plane scheduler that assigns an order to the shipped jobs on a single machine. Preserve its command-line interface: `--input <path>` and `--output-dir <path>`, defaulting to `/app/data/jobs.json` and `/app/output`. The setup matrix and the scheduling policy are always read from their fixed absolute paths, `/app/data/setup_matrix.json` and `/app/data/scheduling_policy.json`; `--input` selects the jobs-and-precedence instance only and never relocates them.

`/app/docs/report_spec.json` is the output contract: file paths, schemas, required-field lists, field coercions, digest payloads and byte-level checksum serialization with worked test vectors. Match it exactly. It does not say how the schedule is computed or what is being minimised.

How the scheduler behaves is not in the contract and you have to reconstruct it. Every governing rule — the time model, the objective, the policy resolution, the precedence handling, the optimisation target, the tie-break between equally good orders and the summary aggregation — was settled by the scheduling governance board, and `/app/incident/scheduling_governance_log.md` is the authoritative record. Most of that log is routine noise, its February drafts were partly reversed and two March interim decisions were revised again in May, so where entries conflict the later dated decision governs. Use the `governing_entry_index` in the report spec to reach the entries that govern each stage instead of reading the log front to back.

The shipped instance is large enough that the search itself has to be engineered. Each full run of the restored scheduler — on the shipped instance and on any alternate instance of comparable size — must complete within 120 seconds of wall-clock time, and the verifier enforces that budget. A textbook enumeration of scheduler states will not finish in it; a pruned one has room to spare.

Write exactly three files into the requested output directory (the `--output-dir`, defaulting to `/app/output`): `schedule.json`, `objective.json` and `summary.json`, each carrying the field set its `required_fields` list in the spec gives.

Do not delegate the optimisation to a third-party solver or graph library and do not build the answer through dynamic code execution; implement it yourself in the standard library. The artifacts must generalize to alternate job instances, stay identical across reruns, and derive only from the operational inputs. Keep the frozen incident snapshot `/app/workflow/.scheduler.original` unchanged, and never read or import verifier artifacts.
