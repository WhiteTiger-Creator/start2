# Batch scheduler optimizer — rollout intrusion containment and repair

Act as the manufacturing-execution / scheduling-platform engineer responding to a failed batch-platform rollout. The rollout automation left persistence and escalation paths on the scheduler host, and it also left the control-plane batch scheduler emitting a stale, greedy job order that is not the order the governance board signed off on.

First contain the host to match the required end-states in `/app/data/containment_checklist.json` — that file is data (a list of required end-states), not a procedure. The rollout staged its persistence under `/app/host`. Revoke the automation's SSH persistence (the line in `/app/host/authorized_keys` carrying the comment `batch-rollout-automation`, leaving any legitimate baseline key in place), remove its passwordless sudoers drop-in `/app/host/sudoers.d/batch-rollout`, and eliminate the exposed world-readable plaintext signing secret in `/app/host/exposed/scheduler_signing.key` so its secret string no longer appears anywhere under `/app`. The scheduler restore does not begin until the host matches every required end-state.

Then restore `/app/workflow/scheduler.py`, the control-plane scheduler that assigns an order to the shipped jobs on a single machine. Preserve its CLI: `--input PATH` and `--output-dir PATH`, defaulting to `/app/data/jobs.json` and `/app/output`. Always read the setup matrix and the scheduling policy from their fixed absolute paths `/app/data/setup_matrix.json` and `/app/data/scheduling_policy.json`; `--input` selects the jobs-and-precedence instance only and never relocates them.

`/app/docs/report_spec.json` is the output contract: file paths, JSON schemas, required-field lists, field coercions, digest payloads and byte-level checksum serialization with worked test vectors. Match it exactly. It does NOT say how the schedule is computed or what the objective is.

How the scheduler behaves is not in the contract and you must reconstruct it. The machine runs one job at a time from time zero. Moving from one job to the next costs a sequence-dependent setup time that depends on the two jobs' families (given by the setup matrix), and each job has a processing time, a due date and a weight. A job is late when it finishes after its (policy-adjusted) due date, and the cost of the whole schedule is built from those weighted latenesses. Some jobs must run before others (the instance's precedence list), and the machine starts configured for a particular family. The board's target is the single best possible order under all of that — the one that makes the total cost as small as it can be — not merely a plausible or greedy order. Because the setup cost depends on which job ran immediately before, the finish times depend on the whole order, so no per-job sort or priority rule yields the board's order in general; the shipped scheduler's greedy heuristic is precedence-feasible but demonstrably not the board's optimum, and reproducing it will not pass.

The governing rules — how completion times accumulate, whether the first job incurs setup and in which order setup and processing apply, how tardiness is graced and weighted, how precedence constrains the order, what exactly is minimised, how ties between equally-good orders are broken, and the policy resolution and checksum serialization — were settled by the scheduling governance board, and `/app/incident/scheduling_governance_log.md` is the authoritative record. Most of that log is routine noise; the February drafts were partly reversed and several March interim decisions were revised again in May, so trace each rule to its FINAL decision — where a draft or interim conflicts with a later decision, the later dated decision governs. Use the `governing_entry_index` in `/app/docs/report_spec.json` to reach the entries that govern each stage rather than reading the log front to back.

Write exactly three files under the requested output directory (the `--output-dir`, defaulting to `/app/output`): `/app/output/schedule.json` (the ordered job sequence, the machine's initial family, and one placement row per job with its setup, start, completion, tardiness and weighted tardiness), `/app/output/objective.json` (the achieved objective and its breakdown, including the ordered sequence), and `/app/output/summary.json` (the full contracted key set including `schema_version`, the per-family weighted-tardiness object and the `*_checksum` digests). Read the spec's `required_fields` lists directly.

The emitted order must be precedence-feasible and must attain the governance objective's proven minimum; among all orders that attain it, emit the one the governance tie-break selects, so the schedule is unique. Do not delegate the optimisation to a third-party solver or graph library and do not build the answer through dynamic code execution — implement the optimisation yourself in the standard library. These artifacts must generalize to alternate job instances, remain idempotent across reruns, and derive only from the operational inputs. Keep the frozen incident snapshot `/app/workflow/.scheduler.original` unchanged and never read or import verifier artifacts.
